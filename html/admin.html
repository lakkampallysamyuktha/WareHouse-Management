Editorial · Reading the control plane

The numbers on this page describe shape, not health.

Carrier count measures network reach; it says nothing about whether those carriers are performing. A carrier can be active and still be failing its lanes — the signals for that live further down this plane, under route performance and open exceptions.

Route count should be read against the region's declared capacity, not against customer demand. A region can carry more routes than it was sized for, and the only way to know is to compare the split here with the capacity envelope in Regional Capacity.

Open exceptions is the one figure on this row that is not a count — it is a backlog. It is the only number that, when it goes up, means someone on the compliance desk is behind. Read it first.

Editorial · Capacity planning notes

Over the declared target is not the same as over capacity.

Every region on this plane carries a declared envelope — the dispatch volume it has committed to serve without degradation. When actual demand crosses the target, three different things could be true, and they are worth distinguishing before reacting.

Sustained overshoot means demand has genuinely grown — the envelope needs to be re-declared and the region resized. Transient spike means a batch, a migration, or a customer event pushed demand briefly above target, and it will likely self-resolve. Measurement drift means the measured demand looks higher than it is because of a reporting change, and resizing on that basis would be a mistake.

Rule of thumb: two consecutive cycles over target → treat as sustained. Anything else → treat as transient until proven otherwise.
